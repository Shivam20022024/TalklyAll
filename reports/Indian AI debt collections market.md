# A Crowded Market Resists Easy Entry

India's AI-powered debt-collections software market already has at least eleven funded, actively-marketing players — Credgenics, DPDzero, Gnani.ai, Spocto X, Rezolv, SquadStack, Trustt, CredResolve, M2P Fintech, Rocketpay, and TruBoard/Elision among them — competing for the same shrinking pool of banks and NBFCs, and the one company with real, independently-reported financial traction (Credgenics: ~₹220 crore FY25 revenue, profitable, ~$340M last disclosed valuation) is already far ahead on scale, logos, and capital ([Entrackr](https://entrackr.com/snippets/credgenics-clocks-rs-200-cr-revenue-and-rs-25-cr-pbt-in-fy25-9357515)). The underlying demand driver is real and structural: unsecured, small-ticket, digitally-originated retail lending is growing far faster than overall bank credit and is where delinquency is concentrating, which genuinely favors scalable AI-first collections over linear headcount growth ([CRIF High Mark](https://crifhighmark.com/news-events/news/2025/jan/personal-loan-growth-slows-to-138-in-h1fy25-long-term-delinquencies-rise-crif); [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/finance/news/nbfc-asset-quality-worsens-to-5-9-amid-rising-write-offs-rbi-fsr-125063001232_1.html)). But no public evidence shows any competitor has a durable technical moat — every vendor's efficiency and recovery-rate claims are self-reported marketing copy with no disclosed methodology, and large lenders appear to multi-source across vendors rather than lock into one, which caps how much any single entrant can extract even if it wins a deal. The regulatory environment is simultaneously the market's biggest entry barrier and its biggest unresolved question: RBI is actively rewriting recovery-agent rules through 2026, individual human DRA certification has no equivalent mechanism for an AI system, and nobody — regulator or vendor — has publicly settled whether a fully autonomous AI voice agent can legally run a collections call without a human in the loop. For Novalantis, the honest read is that this is not a greenfield opportunity: it is a capital-intensive, compliance-heavy, already-contested market where Talkly has none of the specific assets (LMS integrations, promise-to-pay data model, risk scoring, compliance-by-design calling engine, BFSI collections sales motion) that incumbents are building on, and the one credible path in is narrow, not a full-stack assault on Credgenics or DPDzero.

## Eight named rivals, one real moat, and it isn't technology

Across the eight companies named in the brief, the clearest pattern is that differentiation claims cluster around the same handful of talking points — breadth, compliance, latency, outcome-based pricing — and almost none of it is independently verified. **Credgenics** is the category leader by a wide margin: it is the only one of the eight with independently-reported (not just self-published) financials, posting **~₹220 crore FY25 revenue** (up ~40% YoY) and **₹25 crore profit before tax**, on top of **$78M+ raised** across a Series A ($25M) and Series B ($50M at a $340M valuation) ([Entrackr](https://entrackr.com/snippets/credgenics-clocks-rs-200-cr-revenue-and-rs-25-cr-pbt-in-fy25-9357515); [IBS Intelligence](https://ibsintelligence.com/ibsi-news/credgenics-bags-50m-in-series-b-funding-for-sea-expansion)). It sells the broadest bundle in the group — AI voicebot, predictive dialer, field-collections app, litigation/legal tracking (Section 138 cheque-bounce, SARFAESI), and a payments module — and claims 150+ lender clients including logo'd relationships with HDFC, ICICI, Axis, and Kotak ([Credgenics.com](https://credgenics.com/)). That breadth plus audited revenue is the single most defensible position in the set, though its specific performance claims (80% recovery, 5X ROI, 40% cost reduction) carry no disclosed methodology and should be read as marketing, not fact.

**DPDzero** and **Rezolv** are the two credible AI-native challengers, both still early-stage by capital raised (DPDzero ~$10.7-11.8M total; Rezolv $16M total as of its August 2026 Series A) but with more internally consistent press coverage than most of the field ([Entrackr](https://entrackr.com/snippets/dpdzero-raises-7-mn-led-by-gmo-venture-partners-9631116); [YourStory](https://yourstory.com/2026/08/lend-tech-firm-rezolv-raises-12-million-funding-round-led-by-norwest)). DPDzero's differentiation is explicitly structural: it markets itself as "not a SaaS/CRM vendor" but an outcome-based Collections-as-a-Service provider, taking on staffing/BPO-adjacent delivery rather than selling pure software — a different, arguably easier-to-greenlight buying motion than displacing an incumbent's tech stack, but likely thinner margins ([DPDzero platform page](https://dpdzero.com/platform)). Rezolv's edge is founder-market-fit rather than technology: it was built by Kissht's former CTO and COO, who ran an actual NBFC lending book, and it claims (self-reported but consistently repeated across YourStory, Pulse2, and NextBillion) **12M+ loan accounts** and **6.5M borrower-conversation minutes per month** across 22+ bank/NBFC clients ([Pulse2](https://pulse2.com/rezolv-raises-12-5-million-series-a-as-ai-debt-collection-platform-handles-12-million-loan-accounts/)). Press coverage explicitly names Rezolv alongside Credgenics, Nucleus Software, and Mobicule as direct competitors — confirming it is entering an already-populated category, not a gap.

**Gnani.ai** sits structurally apart from the collections specialists: it is a horizontal voice-AI company (ASR/NLU/TTS stack it owns end-to-end) for which "Collect365" is one vertical application alongside customer support and sales, not its core identity. Its most credible differentiator is its February 2026 Razorpay partnership — letting AI voice agents generate and track UPI/payment links mid-call — which is notable because it is one of the only claims in this entire competitive set independently corroborated by two separate outlets rather than resting on vendor self-report alone ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/amp/technology/story/gnaniai-partners-with-razorpay-to-enable-real-time-payment-collections-via-ai-voice-agents-518095-2026-02-26); [The Paypers](https://thepaypers.com/payments/news/gnaniai-and-razorpay-launch-ai-voice-payment-collections-platform)). But Gnani is also the structural lookalike for where Talkly would sit if it entered this market: a general-purpose conversational-AI vendor treating collections as one more vertical, with less collections-specific domain depth than Credgenics or DPDzero — the research notes flag this cuts both ways. **Spocto X**, now owned by the well-capitalized Yubi Group (formerly CredAvenue), differentiates on "agentic AI" branding and institutional scale, but its highest-profile 2025 claims (a "60% recovery boost," ₹50,000 crore in prevented credit losses) were distributed via a paid PTI/NewsVoir wire release that explicitly disclaims editorial responsibility — a markedly weaker evidentiary bar than ordinary tech-press coverage of the other vendors, and it names only one public client (Central Bank of India) despite claiming "50+ financial institutions" ([TheWeek.in/PTI wire](https://www.theweek.in/wire-updates/business/2025/04/03/dcm39-spocto-x.html)).

**SquadStack** and **Trustt** are the weakest fits for "collections specialist." SquadStack is a hybrid AI-plus-human telecalling/CX platform where collections is one use case among sales and customer support; its most specific technical claim — a proprietary speech model, "Arth V2," claimed to beat Sarvam, Deepgram, Google, and OpenAI on 8kHz telephonic audio — is self-reported with no independent benchmark, as is its claim to be "first in the world to pass the Turing Test for contact centers" ([SquadStack](https://www.squadstack.ai/boost-pre-due-collections)). **Trustt** (formerly Novopay) is fundamentally a core-banking/digital-lending platform with HDFC- and YES Bank-caliber logos; its collections "module" is shown only as a dashboard mockup, with no evidence its own AI voice agents actually place collections calls — making it the least verified of the eight as an actual collections-calling competitor, despite having the most prestigious-looking client roster ([trustt.com](https://trustt.com)). The thread running through all eight: large banks and NBFCs demonstrably multi-source (Credgenics and DPDzero both display overlapping HDFC/ICICI/Axis/Tata Capital logos), meaning no single vendor has proven exclusivity-based lock-in — the real competitive sorting is happening on capital, compliance credibility, and integration depth, not on a protectable algorithmic edge.

## Real tailwind, fragmented industry, no sized market

The demand case for AI collections in India rests on a genuine structural bifurcation, not a uniform credit crisis. India's total retail credit book has grown to **~₹162 lakh crore** (December 2025, +18.1% YoY), and headline bank asset quality is at multi-decade lows — system GNPA of **2.1%** as of September 2025, with RBI projecting further improvement to 1.9% by March 2027 ([The Statesman](https://www.thestatesman.com/business/indias-retail-credit-grows-18-yoy-to-rs-162-7-lakh-crore-in-q3-fy26-report-1503562047.html); [ETV Bharat](https://www.etvbharat.com/en/business/banks-npas-to-improve-to-1-dot-9-per-cent-by-march-2027-says-rbi-enn25123103880)). That headline number masks exactly the segment that matters for collections demand: **unsecured retail loans carry a 1.8% GNPA ratio versus 1.1% for overall retail**, account for **~53% of total retail slippages**, and **NBFC microfinance stressed assets jumped from 3.9% to 5.9%** between September 2024 and March 2025 ([adda247](https://currentaffairs.adda247.com/rbis-financial-stability-report-2025-highlights-strong-capital-buffers-rising-risks); [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/finance/news/nbfc-asset-quality-worsens-to-5-9-amid-rising-write-offs-rbi-fsr-125063001232_1.html)). A CRIF/DLAI study found a **44% increase in delinquencies** among personal loans originated between December 2023 and June 2024, and personal loans under ₹10,000 show the highest default rates of any ticket band ([CRIF High Mark](https://crifhighmark.com/news-events/news/2025/jan/personal-loan-growth-slows-to-138-in-h1fy25-long-term-delinquencies-rise-crif); [CRIF High Mark](https://www.crifhighmark.com/news-events/news/2025/jan/personal-loans-under-rs-10-000-record-higher-default-rate)). This is precisely the account profile — high-volume, low-ticket, geographically dispersed, digitally originated — where human-agent collections economics break down and automated calling has the strongest structural case.

The volume driving this is itself growing fast: **digital NBFCs accounted for ~80% of personal loan volumes** in Q1 FY26, while representing a much smaller share of loan value (digital loans are ~78% of volume but only ~19% of value, confirming they skew small-ticket), and BCG/QED's Global Fintech 2025 report cites fintech-led digital lending growing at a **35% CAGR** in India ([IANSLive](https://ianslive.in/digital-nbfcs-account-for-80-pc-of-personal-loan-volumes-in-q1-fy26-report--20251014131831); [The Hans India](https://thehansindia.com/business/india-stands-out-as-a-global-fintech-bright-spot-as-credit-demand-surges-bcg-report-976453)). Note this 35% figure is a broad digital-lending growth estimate, not a collections-market forecast — no analyst house (Redseer, BCG, PwC, Bain) has published a direct TAM figure for India's collections-tech segment specifically; this is a real, confirmed gap in the public record. What can be said with more confidence is that recently-funded, venture-backed AI-collections startups alone (DPDzero $7M, Rezolv $12.5M of $16M total, CredResolve's smaller round) sum to a rough, incomplete floor of **$20-25M+ in disclosed 2025-2026 VC funding** into named players — almost certainly an undercount since it excludes Credgenics' and M2P's funding histories and any undisclosed rounds. The vendor-claimed cost case for AI over human telecalling (quoted figures range wildly from ₹12-29 per AI-resolved contact up to ₹150-200 per human interaction, depending on which vendor's blog you read) is directionally plausible but entirely unaudited — treat any specific percentage savings figure from any vendor, including prospective Talkly pitches, as a claim to be proven per-deployment, not an industry constant ([caller.digital](https://caller.digital/blog/cost-per-resolved-contact-chat-vs-voice-ai-india-2026)).

## Regulation is the moat incumbents are racing to build

The single most consequential finding for a horizontal voice-AI vendor considering this market is that India's rules for debt-collection calling are being actively rewritten in real time, and the existing certification regime was built for humans in a way that has no clean analogue for an AI system. RBI's long-standing Fair Practices Code bars intimidation and sets an **8am–7pm calling window**, reinforced by an August 2022 circular (RBI/2022-23/108) extending the rule across banks, cooperative banks, and non-bank lenders ([Business Standard](https://business-standard.com/amp/article/finance/rbi-directs-loan-recovery-agents-not-to-intimidate-borrowers-no-calling-before-8am-after-7pm-122081201144_1.html)). That framework is now being superseded by a much more prescriptive "Responsible Business Conduct" regime, reportedly moving through draft stages in February, May, and August 2026, which — per secondary legal commentary, since the primary RBI text could not be independently retrieved in this research — adds mandatory call-recording disclosure, a minimum **6-month recording retention**, a hard rule that agents may contact **only the borrower or guarantor** (not relatives, employers, or references without consent), and new restrictions on remote device-locking tied to DPD thresholds ([Vinod Kothari Consultants](https://vinodkothari.com/2026/08/rbis-recovery-norms-allow-device-locking-by-lenders/); [Sansa Legal](https://www.sansalegal.com/post/rbi-responsible-business-conduct-directions-2026-new-rules-on-debt-recovery-and-borrower-rights)). Note that different secondary sources disagree on retention periods (6 months vs. 90 days vs. 3 years cited across different vendor blogs) and on effective dates — this is genuinely unsettled, not a matter of research gaps on our end, and would need direct legal verification against rbi.org.in before any product commitment.

The harder structural problem is certification. RBI requires human recovery agents to hold an **IIBF Debt Recovery Agent (DRA) certification** — a personal credential earned through a 50-100 hour course — and 2026 provisions reportedly extend this requirement across all regulated entities with only a one-year grace period for existing uncertified agents ([Careers360](https://www.careers360.com/courses-certifications/articles/iibf-dra-certificate); [Vinod Kothari](https://vinodkothari.com/2026/08/rbis-recovery-norms-allow-device-locking-by-lenders/)). No RBI document found in this research explicitly names AI, voicebots, or automated calling at all — the rules are written around conduct outcomes (no harassment, correct hours, proper disclosure) rather than caller identity, which gives a plausible but unconfirmed argument that AI calling isn't per se prohibited. But because DRA certification is structured as an individual human credential with no equivalent mechanism for a software agent, the safest reading — not a confirmed legal position, but the prudent default — is that an AI voice system should be built as a tool operating under a certified human's accountability, with DPD-based escalation (AI leading early-stage 0-30 DPD contact, human-led by 60-90 DPD) rather than as an autonomous substitute for a certified agent. On top of RBI's rules sits the DPDP Act 2023, which treats collections call recordings as personal data requiring purpose-specific consent distinct from RBI's own retention mandates — meaning a compliant system needs two parallel, non-substitutable compliance tracks (DPDP consent/erasure logic and RBI retention/disclosure logic) running simultaneously, and conflicting sources put DPDP's enforcement date anywhere from November 2026 to May 2027 ([Khaitan & Co.](https://compass.khaitanco.com/consent-architecture-in-financial-services-distribution-comparing-rbis-responsible-business-conduct-directions-and-the-dpdp-framework)). Every incumbent markets "compliance by design" (DND/time-window enforcement, consent logging, ISO 27001 certification) as a differentiator, but none of this is independently audited or regulator-verified — which means compliance credibility in this market is currently built through sales trust and contractual indemnification with lenders, not through any publicly verifiable certification, and a new entrant starts with zero of that trust.

## What a real product would need that Talkly doesn't have today

Four capabilities separate a genuine collections-intelligence platform from a general-purpose calling bot pointed at a delinquency list, and the research found all four are harder and more specific than they first appear. **LMS integration** is not a standard API: Indian lenders run a fragmented mix of SaaS platforms (Finflux/M2P, LendFoundry-descended systems) and bespoke in-house or core-banking builds (Finacle-class), so each lender onboarding is a partly bespoke integration project — pulling loan ID, DPD, outstanding balance, repayment schedule, and payment history, and pushing back payment confirmations and disposition status ([EngineerBabu](https://engineerbabu.com/blog/?p=21686); [Credgenics API docs](https://providers.apievangelist.com/providers/credgenics/)). Vendor claims of "integration in days" (DPDzero) should be read as the best case where a lender already has clean, documented APIs, not the norm.

**Promise-to-pay (PTP) tracking** is a specific, hard AI-voice engineering problem, not a generic CRM note field: it requires robust date/amount extraction from accented, code-switched speech in real time, a conversational branch to distinguish a genuine payment commitment from a callback request or a vague deferral, an explicit on-call confirmation turn, and — critically — reconciliation of the "promise" against actual payment-system data rather than trusting the call transcript, since a verbal promise is not proof of payment. This needs its own structured data model (promise ID, loan ID, promised date/amount, channel, confirmation status, fulfillment status) distinct from generic call disposition logs. **Borrower segmentation/scoring** done properly separates a statistical risk/propensity score from a policy layer that converts the score into contact strategy, with evidence that bucket-specific models (early/mid/late DPD) outperform one generic score — this requires ongoing feature engineering (payment recency, DPD trend, PTP fulfillment history) and model-drift monitoring, meaningfully more data-engineering investment than "sort by days overdue" ([fitgap.com](https://us.fitgap.com/stack-guides/automate-delinquency-segmentation-and-negotiation-policies-with-behavior-based-risk-scoring); [Experian](https://cer.business-school.ed.ac.uk/wp-content/uploads/sites/55/2017/03/Scoring-Models-for-Collections-and-Debt-Recovery-Deuchars.pdf)). **Recovery analytics** turns out to be mostly a reporting/BI layer over the same underlying scoring and PTP data — not a separate ML capability — but no vendor surveyed discloses cost-per-recovery or true channel-attribution methodology, which is one of the few genuinely open differentiation gaps in the market: a transparent, methodologically disclosed analytics layer would be a real point of difference, because every incumbent currently hides behind unaudited aggregate claims. Finally, **compliance-by-design calling** is not a checkbox but a living rules engine: a hard-gated 8am-7pm scheduler per borrower locale, tamper-evident recording with configurable (and currently disputed) retention, DPD-aware AI-to-human handoff, grievance-language detection routing to a certified human officer, TRAI DLT/principal-entity registration for automated outbound calls, and dual-track DPDP/RBI consent logging — built as configurable, auditable parameters per lender and loan type, because the regulatory text itself is still moving.

## The verdict: not a good general expansion target, a narrow one at best

Taken together, the case against a full-scale Novalantis entry into this market right now is stronger than the case for it. This is not a greenfield category: it has at least eleven funded, actively competing vendors, one of which (Credgenics) has real audited revenue, 150+ lender relationships, and a $340M valuation, and two more (DPDzero, Rezolv) with credible founder-market-fit and multi-million-dollar war chests purpose-built for this fight. Talkly brings none of the four specific capabilities that matter here — no LMS integration layer, no PTP data model, no collections-specific scoring/segmentation, no compliance-by-design calling engine — and building all four to a credible standard is a multi-quarter engineering investment, not a configuration change on top of the existing real-estate/healthcare/edtech/BFSI-sales product. The compliance bar is also qualitatively different from Talkly's current calling: a bad call in real estate or edtech lead-gen is a lost lead; a bad call in debt collections is a potential RBI Ombudsman complaint against the lender, who will contractually push that liability back onto Talkly, and the IIBF-DRA certification gap means a human-accountability layer is probably mandatory, not optional, undercutting the pure-automation pitch that makes AI voice economically attractive in the first place. The buyer is also a different animal: collections/risk heads at banks and NBFCs, not the sales or ops stakeholders Talkly currently sells to, requiring a new enterprise motion, security diligence (ISO 27001-equivalent at minimum), and reference-able case studies Talkly doesn't yet have in BFSI collections specifically — its existing "BFSI sales" vertical does not transfer trust to a collections buyer, who cares about harassment risk and regulatory exposure, not conversion rate.

That said, the fact that large lenders demonstrably multi-source across vendors (Credgenics and DPDzero share overlapping bank logos) means this market doesn't require unseating an incumbent to get a toehold — coexistence is the observed norm, not winner-take-all. If Novalantis wants a real foothold rather than a distraction, the defensible entry point is narrow: **early-stage (0-30 DPD) pre-due and reminder calling**, not full-lifecycle recovery. That segment is structurally closest to what Talkly already does well — outbound, high-volume, AI-led conversational calling with lower stakes per call — carries materially lower regulatory and reputational risk than mid-to-late-stage recovery (no IIBF-certification tension yet, since this is reminder/servicing-adjacent rather than enforcement), and several vendor compliance frameworks explicitly treat this band as AI-appropriate before human negotiators take over at 30+ DPD. Pursuing that beachhead would still require building a real PTP data model, basic DPD-aware segmentation, and genuine LMS read-access for at least one or two design-partner lenders — not the full stack — plus a dedicated compliance-by-design calling engine, since that is non-negotiable table stakes even for early-stage reminders. Absent a specific lender relationship or a committed multi-quarter build budget earmarked for this vertical, the better use of founder time is to treat debt collections as a watch-item, not a 2026-2027 roadmap priority: revisit if a design-partner NBFC or bank approaches Talkly directly, since inbound demand would solve the two hardest problems (initial compliance trust and a reference logo) that an outbound push into this market cannot solve quickly on its own.

## Conclusion

The research surfaces a market that looks attractive from the outside — real credit growth, real delinquency, real vendor funding activity — but whose actual competitive dynamics are set by compliance trust and integration depth accumulated over years, not by any AI technology Talkly could plausibly out-build in the near term; nobody in this space, including the leaders, has a technical moat that survives scrutiny, which is oddly the most honest argument *against* rushing in, since it means the winners so far have won on capital, founder-market-fit, and sales trust rather than product superiority Talkly could leapfrog. The more durable insight is that India's debt-collections AI market and India's RBI-regulated-calling compliance burden are now effectively the same problem: any AI voice vendor serious about BFSI collections is, in practice, building a regulatory-compliance product with a voice interface bolted on, and that is a fundamentally different company than a conversational-AI SaaS platform serving real estate and edtech leads — which is exactly why the honest verdict here is to wait for a specific, inbound reason to enter rather than manufacture one.
