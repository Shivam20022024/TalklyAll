# Market size and growth drivers for AI-based/digital debt collections in India

## What is the size of India's retail/consumer lending book, NPA/stressed-asset volume, and how has it trended recently?

### Takeaway
India's retail credit book has grown to roughly ₹162 lakh crore (~Dec 2025) at ~18% YoY, even as headline bank asset quality has improved to multi-decade-low GNPA ratios (~2.1%); the real stress is concentrated and rising specifically in unsecured retail (personal loans/credit cards) and NBFC microfinance, which is the segment most relevant to collections demand.

### Cited Findings
- Total outstanding retail loans in India reached ~₹162 lakh crore in the December 2025 quarter, up 18.1% YoY, per a CRIF High Mark report — [The Statesman](https://www.thestatesman.com/business/indias-retail-credit-grows-18-yoy-to-rs-162-7-lakh-crore-in-q3-fy26-report-1503562047.html); [Devdiscourse](https://www.devdiscourse.com/article/headlines/3815858-outstanding-retail-loans-up-18-pc-to-rs-162-lakh-cr-in-q3-report)
- Within that, gold loans grew 44.1% YoY to ₹16.2 lakh crore, personal loans grew 11.6% YoY to ₹15.9 lakh crore, and home loans grew 10.5% to ₹43 lakh crore (same Dec-2025-quarter CRIF data) — [The Statesman](https://www.thestatesman.com/business/indias-retail-credit-grows-18-yoy-to-rs-162-7-lakh-crore-in-q3-fy26-report-1503562047.html)
- Conflicting retail-credit totals for September 2025: one report cites ~₹144 lakh crore outstanding, another cites ~₹156 lakh crore (+18% YoY) for the same period — likely different definitions/scope; treat as approximate — [Tribune India](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/business/indias-retail-credit-grows-steadily-at-18-in-q2fy26-led-by-gold-auto-loans-crif); [search aggregation, unverified primary]
- System-wide bank GNPA ratio stood at 2.1% as of September 2025, a multi-decade low — [RBI FSR coverage via search aggregation](https://currentaffairs.adda247.com/rbis-financial-stability-report-2025-highlights-strong-capital-buffers-rising-risks)
- Banks' gross NPAs in absolute terms were ₹4.32 lakh crore in FY24-25, down from ₹4.81 lakh crore in FY23-24 — [search aggregation of RBI FSR coverage]
- Public sector banks' gross NPAs were ₹2.84 lakh crore (2.58% of total loans) by March 2025 — [Angel One](https://www.angelone.in/news/economy/rbi-drives-psb-npa-reduction-in-2025)
- Banks wrote off ~₹1.58 lakh crore in FY25, with actual recoveries of only ~₹0.68 lakh crore — [search aggregation, source article not individually confirmed]
- RBI's Financial Stability Report (editions covering March 2025 and September 2025 data) puts the GNPA ratio for unsecured retail loans at 1.8%, versus 1.1% for overall retail advances — [studyiq.com](https://www.studyiq.com/articles/rbi-financial-stability-report); [adda247](https://currentaffairs.adda247.com/rbis-financial-stability-report-2025-highlights-strong-capital-buffers-rising-risks)
- Unsecured retail loans (mainly personal loans and credit cards) accounted for ~53% of total retail loan slippages in the period covered by the FSR — [adda247](https://currentaffairs.adda247.com/rbis-financial-stability-report-2025-highlights-strong-capital-buffers-rising-risks)
- Private-sector banks contributed ~76% of total unsecured retail slippages, versus ~16% for public-sector banks — [adda247](https://currentaffairs.adda247.com/rbis-financial-stability-report-2025-highlights-strong-capital-buffers-rising-risks)
- NBFC microfinance-sector stressed assets (incl. NBFC-MFIs) rose to 5.9% in March 2025, up from 3.9% in September 2024 — [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/finance/news/nbfc-asset-quality-worsens-to-5-9-amid-rising-write-offs-rbi-fsr-125063001232_1.html)
- RBI flagged that banks are increasingly acquiring NBFC-originated retail loans to grow their books, with ~80% of such acquired assets sourced from a small number of large NBFCs — [search aggregation of RBI FSR coverage]
- RBI projects banks' aggregate GNPA ratio to improve further to 1.9% by March 2027 under its baseline scenario — [ETV Bharat](https://www.etvbharat.com/en/business/banks-npas-to-improve-to-1-dot-9-per-cent-by-march-2027-says-rbi-enn25123103880)

### Inferences
- The aggregate/headline NPA picture (improving, multi-decade lows) masks a bifurcated reality: secured, large-ticket retail (home, gold, auto) is healthy, while unsecured small-ticket retail and NBFC-MFI books are seeing rising stress — this is precisely the segment (high volume, low ticket, geographically dispersed, digitally originated) where human-agent-based collections is least economical and AI/digital-first collections has the strongest cost/scale case.
- The scale of the retail book (~₹162 lakh crore) combined with even a low single-digit delinquency rate implies several lakh crore rupees of accounts requiring active collections follow-up at any time, though no source gives a precise "total loans currently in collections" figure for India.

### Gaps
- No single, dated, primary RBI Financial Stability Report figure was directly retrieved (fetches to RBI's own PDF were not attempted/available); all NPA/FSR figures above come from secondary press/aggregator coverage of the report and could not be cross-checked against the primary PDF in this research pass.
- No source gives a consolidated "total NPA/stressed asset value in rupees" across banks + NBFCs + digital lenders combined for 2025 — figures are fragmented by lender type and period.
- No figure found for the total rupee value or account count of loans actively "in collections" (as opposed to formally NPA) in India — this is the more directly relevant TAM driver for collections-tech but wasn't available in any source found.

## How fast has NBFC and fintech/digital lending grown in India, and what does that imply for collections volume?

### Takeaway
NBFC and digital/fintech lending have both grown materially faster than bank credit in recent years (NBFC credit CAGR ~14-17% vs bank ~11-12%; digital lending CAGR as high as ~35% by one estimate), with digital/NBFC channels now originating the large majority of personal loans by volume — directly expanding the pool of small-ticket, high-volume accounts that need scalable (i.e., digital/AI) collections.

### Cited Findings
- NBFC credit has grown at a 13.9% CAGR versus banks' 11.4% CAGR since FY20, per one analysis — [search aggregation]
- Nomura forecasts NBFC credit will grow at ~17% annually between FY25 and FY35, versus ~12% for bank lending over the same period, citing AI as a transformation driver for non-bank lenders — [Lokmat Times](https://www.lokmattimes.com/business/indias-non-bank-lenders-seen-growing-faster-than-banks-as-ai-transforms-lending-nomura-report/); [Newsable](https://newsable.asianetnews.com/amp/business/nbfcs-to-outpace-banks-with-17-growth-over-next-decade-nomura-articleshow-zyf38ur)
- NBFC retail credit portfolio expanded at a 23% CAGR during FY23-FY24 — [search aggregation]
- Digital NBFCs accounted for ~80% of personal loan volumes in Q1 FY26, per one industry report — [IANSLive](https://ianslive.in/digital-nbfcs-account-for-80-pc-of-personal-loan-volumes-in-q1-fy26-report--20251014131831)
- A separate figure: digital loans represented 78% of all personal loans by volume but only 19% by value (i.e., digital loans are disproportionately small-ticket) — [search aggregation]
- Digital NBFC personal-loan portfolio reached ~₹1.2 lakh crore as of June 2025, per CRIF High Mark data — [search aggregation]
- In H1 FY26 (per FACE — Fintech Association for Consumer Empowerment), digital lending sanctions reached ₹97,381 crore across 6.4 crore loans, with digital personal loans representing ~80% of sanction volume; average ticket size rose from ₹13,327 to ₹15,177 YoY — [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/personal-finance/news/story/digital-lending-rises-rs-97381-cr-sanctioned-in-digital-loans-as-online-lending-captures-80-market-share-504840-2025-12-03)
- Digital lending volumes grew 131% from 3.10 crore loans disbursed in FY21-22 to 7.26 crore loans in FY22-23, per one industry report (note: RBI guidelines caused a ~10% dip in one later quarter) — [search aggregation]
- Digital personal loans outstanding crossed ₹1.39 lakh crore, up 53%, per a FACE report (exact reference period unclear/not independently confirmed) — [Moneylife](https://moneylife.in/article/digital-personal-loans-surge-53-percentage-outstanding-crosses-139-lakh-crore-face-report/80043.html)
- BCG and QED Investors' "Global Fintech 2025" report states fintech-led digital lending is growing at a 35% CAGR in India, and expects fintechs to keep leading credit innovation via AI and risk-layered product design — [The Hans India](https://thehansindia.com/business/india-stands-out-as-a-global-fintech-bright-spot-as-credit-demand-surges-bcg-report-976453)
- RBI consolidated digital lending regulation via the "Reserve Bank of India (Digital Lending) Directions, 2025" (circular RBI/2025-26/36, dated 8 May 2025) — [search aggregation, regulatory reference]
- India has roughly 220 million loan and credit card accounts in aggregate, cited as market context in coverage of AI collections startup Rezolv — [Pulse2](https://pulse2.com/rezolv-raises-12-5-million-series-a-as-ai-debt-collection-platform-handles-12-million-loan-accounts/) (note: this figure's original/primary source is not identified; treat as a secondary citation)

### Inferences
- The combination of (a) NBFC/digital credit growing 1.5-2x the rate of bank credit, and (b) digital channels already dominating personal-loan volume (≥78-80%) while representing a much smaller share of value, means the number of discrete small-ticket accounts needing collections touchpoints is growing far faster than the rupee value of the book — a volume problem that favors automated/AI-first collections over linear headcount scaling.
- Estimate/extrapolation: if digital lending volume continues anywhere near the 35% CAGR cited by BCG/QED, the number of accounts requiring collections outreach in any given delinquency bucket would roughly double every ~2.3 years, implying collections infrastructure must scale at a similar or faster pace just to hold delinquency rates constant.

### Gaps
- No single authoritative, RBI-sourced time series for "digital lending disbursement value/volume, FY22-FY26" was found; figures are drawn from multiple industry reports (FACE, CRIF, BCG/QED) with different scopes, time periods, and methodologies, so they should not be chained into one growth curve.
- Could not verify the ₹1.39 lakh crore / 53% growth figure's reference period or confirm it against a primary FACE report document.
- No figure found for the absolute count of "collections accounts" generated by NBFC/fintech growth specifically (as opposed to total loan accounts or disbursement volume).

## What is driving demand for AI-powered/digital-first collections specifically?

### Takeaway
Demand is driven by a combination of rising delinquency in unsecured/small-ticket digital loans, the sheer volume/dispersion of small-ticket accounts that make traditional agent-based collections uneconomical, and claimed (vendor-sourced, not independently audited) cost savings of AI voice/chat collections versus human telecallers or BPO seats; the traditional collections industry is also described as highly fragmented and manual, creating a modernization opportunity.

### Cited Findings
- A CRIF High Mark / DLAI (Digital Lenders Association of India) report found a 44% increase in delinquencies among personal loan borrowers whose loans originated between December 2023 and June 2024 — [CRIF High Mark](https://crifhighmark.com/news-events/news/2025/jan/personal-loan-growth-slows-to-138-in-h1fy25-long-term-delinquencies-rise-crif)
- Personal loan YoY growth decelerated sharply, from 29.1% in September 2023 to 13.8% in September 2024, per CRIF — [CRIF High Mark](https://crifhighmark.com/news-events/news/2025/jan/personal-loan-growth-slows-to-138-in-h1fy25-long-term-delinquencies-rise-crif)
- A July 2025 report found fintech personal loan defaults hit a six-quarter high, with originations from tier-3 cities and beyond driving most of the rise; the 90+ day default rate for this segment was 4.2% — [Moneylife](https://moneylife.in/article/fintech-personal-loan-defaults-hit-6quarter-high-driven-by-young-borrowers-and-small-towns/77554.html)
- CRIF's "How India Lends" FY25 report found personal loan originations declined 2.9% in value to ₹8.8 lakh crore — [CRIF High Mark](https://www.crifhighmark.com/media/5038/how-india-lends-report.pdf) (PDF link found via search; content not independently fetched/verified)
- As of October 2025, delinquency and deep-stage stress remained elevated even after the RBI's clampdown on unsecured lending, per Mint coverage — [search aggregation]
- A January 2026 CRIF demographic report (using data as of November 2025) found borrowers under 30 show higher delinquency than other age groups on unsecured and vehicle loans, even as overall delinquency improved YoY — [Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/business/borrowers-under-30-show-higher-delinquency-in-retail-loans-crif-report)
- Personal loans under ₹10,000 recorded a higher default rate than larger-ticket personal loans, per CRIF — [CRIF High Mark](https://www.crifhighmark.com/news-events/news/2025/jan/personal-loans-under-rs-10-000-record-higher-default-rate)
- DPDzero's funding coverage frames the Indian collections market as fragmented, split across "thousands of independent agencies using manual processes and limited technology" — [search aggregation of Inc42/YourStory coverage]
- Vendor-sourced cost estimates (marketing-grade, not audited): a tier-2-city BPO collections seat costs ~₹18,000-25,000/month, equating to roughly ₹7-13 per connected call, per one AI-voice vendor — [ecorpit.com](https://ecorpit.com/ecorpit-ai-voice-agent-development-service-india-2026/)
- Another vendor estimates human telecaller cost at ₹60-130 per voice contact, versus ₹12-29 per AI-resolved contact — [caller.digital](https://caller.digital/blog/cost-per-resolved-contact-chat-vs-voice-ai-india-2026)
- A third vendor (for an insurance use case) argues realistic human cost is ₹150-200 per interaction, pushing back on commonly-quoted ₹30-50 figures as too low — [cyfuture.ai](https://cyfuture.ai/blog/ai-voicebot-for-insurance-how-bfsi-companies-cut-call-costs-by-60)
- One AI voice vendor (Oriserve) claims ~30% lower cost per outcome versus a BPO seat, and up to 80% lower per interaction when AI handles the full call end-to-end — [oriserve.com](https://oriserve.com/blog/answers) (vendor claim, not independently verified)
- A single customer testimonial on a vendor's site claims 70% lower cost versus a previous BPO partner — [edesy.in](https://edesy.in/ai-voice-agent/use-cases/debt-collection) (single anecdote, not audited data)
- Caller.digital itself cautions that at ₹6/minute AI voice pricing, a 3-minute collections call (₹18) can exceed the cost of an equivalent tier-2 BPO agent call, and warns that per-minute AI pricing structurally rewards longer calls — [caller.digital](https://caller.digital/blog/cost-per-resolved-contact-chat-vs-voice-ai-india-2026)
- Rezolv (AI collections platform) claims a 35% improvement in resolution rate for lenders using its platform (company-reported claim) — [Pulse2](https://pulse2.com/rezolv-raises-12-5-million-series-a-as-ai-debt-collection-platform-handles-12-million-loan-accounts/)

### Inferences
- The "AI is cheaper than human collections" narrative is currently supported almost entirely by vendor marketing content rather than independent/audited benchmarks; the spread of quoted human costs (₹30-200+ per interaction) is wide enough that the real savings from AI likely vary enormously by use case, call length, and automation rate — this should be flagged as directional, not a hard number, in any report.
- Rising delinquency concentrated in small-ticket (<₹10,000), young, tier-3+-city, fintech-originated personal loans is a strong structural driver for AI-first collections specifically, because these are the accounts where per-account collection economics are worst for human-agent models (low recovery value per account relative to agent cost) and where digital-native borrowers are more amenable to chat/voice-bot outreach.

### Gaps
- No independently audited (non-vendor) cost comparison between human and AI-based collections in the Indian market was found.
- No data found on the actual automation/AI-driven recovery rate improvement across the industry as a whole (only individual vendor claims for their own platforms).

## Is there market-size or funding data specifically on the "collections-as-a-service" / collections-tech software segment in India?

### Takeaway
No credible third-party TAM estimate for India's collections-tech/collections-as-a-service segment was found; what exists is funding data on a handful of named startups (DPDzero, Rezolv, CredResolve, plus established players Credgenics, M2P Fintech, Rocketpay) showing the segment is young, venture-backed, and growing, but aggregate sector funding and vendor-count figures are not available from any source located.

### Cited Findings
- DPDzero (Bengaluru; founded 2022 by Ananth Shroff and Ranjith Ramachandra) raised a $7 million Series A (~₹60 crore) in August 2025, led by GMO Venture Partners, with India Quotient, Sinarmas Group, SMBC Asia Rising Fund, and existing investor Blume Ventures participating — [Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/debt-collection-startup-dpdzero-nets-7-mn-from-gmo-venture-partners-others); [Entrepreneur India](https://india.entrepreneur.com/?p=84303); [CB Insights date: 5 Aug 2025]
- DPDzero operates on a commission model (earns a percentage of amounts collected); funds are earmarked for a multilingual AI collection agent and a field-agent network starting in Bengaluru and expanding to Maharashtra and Uttar Pradesh — [search aggregation of YourStory/Inc42 coverage]
- DPDzero self-reports clients including L&T Finance, Tata Capital, Moneyview, and Paysense (company-reported, not independently verified) — [search aggregation]
- Rezolv (Mumbai; founded 2024 by Sonali Jindal and Karan Mehta, both ex-Kissht) raised a $12.5 million Series A led by Norwest Venture Partners, with Vertex Ventures and 3one4 Capital participating, bringing total funding to $16 million (round reported August 2026) — [Pulse2](https://pulse2.com/rezolv-raises-12-5-million-series-a-as-ai-debt-collection-platform-handles-12-million-loan-accounts/); [fintech.global](https://fintech.global/2026/08/20/rezolv-bags-12-5m-to-scale-ai-led-debt-collection/)
- Rezolv's AI collections platform serves 12+ million loan accounts, handles 6.5+ million minutes of borrower conversations per month, works with 22+ banks/NBFCs, operates in 11 Indian languages, and claims a 35% resolution-rate improvement for lenders — [Pulse2](https://pulse2.com/rezolv-raises-12-5-million-series-a-as-ai-debt-collection-platform-handles-12-million-loan-accounts/)
- CredResolve (Gurugram-based) raised a pre-Series A round led by Merak Ventures, with existing investors Unleash Capital Partners and CDM Capital; the amount is reported as undisclosed in one source, while another source puts total funding at ~₹11 crore (reported as ~$1.1M seed previously) — [Entrackr](https://entrackr.com/snippets/credresolve-raises-11-mn-in-seed-round-8773410); [Ventureintelligence](https://news.ventureintelligence.com/private-equity/credresolve-raises-pre-series-a-funding-led-by-merak-ventures) — note: figures across sources are inconsistent and should be treated cautiously
- Other named vendors in the space (self-described, not independently sized): Credgenics (serves banks, NBFCs, fintechs, and asset reconstruction companies / ARCs), M2P Fintech ("India's leading full-suite debt collection SaaS platform" per company description), Rocketpay (self-described as building "India's collections infrastructure") — [m2pfintech.com](https://m2pfintech.com/debt-collections); [rocketpay.co.in](https://www.rocketpay.co.in/about)

### Inferences
- The presence of at least 3 recently-funded, venture-backed AI-native collections startups (DPDzero, Rezolv, CredResolve) plus established enterprise vendors (Credgenics, M2P) indicates active, multi-player market formation over 2022-2026, consistent with investors treating collections-tech as an emerging distinct category within Indian fintech infrastructure — but this is an inference from funding events, not a market-size calculation.
- Estimate/extrapolation: summing only the disclosed rounds found (DPDzero $7M, Rezolv $12.5M of $16M total, CredResolve ~$1.1M+undisclosed) gives a lower-bound of roughly $20-25M+ in disclosed VC funding into named India-focused AI-collections startups across 2025-2026 alone; this is almost certainly incomplete (excludes earlier/undisclosed rounds, excludes non-headline players, and excludes enterprise vendors like Credgenics/M2P whose funding history wasn't retrieved in this pass) and should be presented only as a rough, partial floor, not a sector total.

### Gaps
- No TAM/market-size estimate (in dollars or rupees) specifically for India's collections-as-a-service or collections-tech software segment was found from any analyst firm (Redseer, BCG, PwC, Bain, or others) — searches for this specifically returned no dedicated report.
- No count of the total number of collections-tech vendors operating in India was found (only a qualitative characterization of "thousands of independent [traditional] agencies").
- Credgenics' and M2P Fintech's own funding histories/valuations were not retrieved in this research pass — a follow-up search specifically on "Credgenics funding valuation" would likely close this gap.
- Could not resolve the conflicting CredResolve funding figures (undisclosed pre-Series A vs. ~₹11 crore/~$1.1M total) — flagging the discrepancy rather than picking one.

## What do industry analysts, RBI, or fintech publications forecast for growth of this segment over the next few years?

### Takeaway
No analyst forecast speaks directly to the collections-tech segment's future size, but adjacent forecasts — NBFC credit growth outpacing banks through FY35, continued digital-lending CAGR in the 30%+ range, and RBI's own GNPA projections — all point to a growing underlying base of credit (and likely delinquent accounts) that the collections-tech sector would serve; these should be read as indirect, extrapolated signals rather than direct collections-market forecasts.

### Cited Findings
- Nomura forecasts NBFC credit to grow at ~17% CAGR from FY25 to FY35, versus ~12% for bank credit, explicitly citing AI as transforming non-bank lending — [Lokmat Times](https://www.lokmattimes.com/business/indias-non-bank-lenders-seen-growing-faster-than-banks-as-ai-transforms-lending-nomura-report/)
- RBI projects the banking system's aggregate GNPA ratio to improve to 1.9% by March 2027 (baseline macro scenario) — [ETV Bharat](https://www.etvbharat.com/en/business/banks-npas-to-improve-to-1-dot-9-per-cent-by-march-2027-says-rbi-enn25123103880)
- BCG and QED Investors' "Global Fintech 2025" report names India as one of the most dynamic global fintech markets and a top destination for future fintech investment, forecasting continued fintech leadership in credit innovation through AI and risk-layered product design, with India's fintech-led digital lending growing at a 35% CAGR — [The Hans India](https://thehansindia.com/business/india-stands-out-as-a-global-fintech-bright-spot-as-credit-demand-surges-bcg-report-976453)
- BCG's "Convergence: Human + AI for the Next Era of Finance" (Global Fintech Fest 2025 flagship report) includes a chapter specifically on AI in Indian finance, though no specific collections-sector figures were retrievable from available excerpts — [BCG](https://www.bcg.com/publications/2025/convergence-human-ai-finance-era); [PDF](https://web-assets.bcg.com/f7/02/5b83b0c64ed08175001763448984/convergence-human-ai-for-the-next-era-of-finance.pdf)
- Inc42's "State of Indian Fintech Report H1 2025" (with an "AI Rewiring India's Lending Playbook" focus) reports digital lending startups attracted the highest share (37%) of Indian fintech funding from 2020 through H1 2025, and the report's framing explicitly covers AI's growing role in underwriting, disbursement, and recovery/collections — [Inc42](https://inc42.com/?p=523572)
- Ken Research estimates the (broadly defined) India digital lending market at USD 491 billion in 2025, projected to reach USD 1,106 billion by 2031 — flagged explicitly as a market-research estimate using a broad definition of "digital lending," not a collections-specific figure and not an RBI or primary source — [Ken Research](https://www.kenresearch.com/india-digital-lending-market)
- PwC India's FinTech Deals Tracker (October 2025) counts 30 FinTech unicorns in India as of 30 June 2025, concentrated in payments and lending — [PwC-sourced, via search aggregation]

### Inferences
- Because every growth forecast located is about lending/credit broadly (NBFC credit, digital lending, fintech funding) rather than collections specifically, any projection of collections-tech market growth must be treated as an extrapolation: if delinquency rates on a faster-growing, higher-risk (unsecured, small-ticket) loan base stay even roughly constant, the absolute number of accounts requiring collections activity should grow at a rate approaching the underlying credit growth rate (double-digit to mid-30s% CAGR by various estimates) — this is an inference, not a sourced collections-market forecast.
- The fact that RBI itself is forecasting improving GNPA ratios while NBFC-MFI stress is explicitly called out as rising in the same reporting period suggests analysts and the regulator may not be aligned on whether unsecured/microfinance stress is "under control" — a nuance a report should preserve rather than averaging away.

### Gaps
- No analyst house (Redseer, BCG, PwC, Bain, McKinsey, or similar) was found publishing a direct forecast ("India's collections-tech/collections-as-a-service market will reach $X by 20XX") — this specific figure appears not to exist publicly, or was not surfaced by the searches run in this pass.
- Could not independently access/verify the full text of the BCG "Convergence" report or the Inc42 "State of Indian Fintech H1 2025" report beyond search-engine summaries; a researcher with direct access to these paywalled/full reports could likely extract more specific collections-relevant forecasts.
