# Regulatory and Compliance Landscape for AI-Powered Debt Collection Calling in India

**IMPORTANT FRAMING NOTE:** This document is a research synthesis for business planning only, not legal advice. The Indian regulatory framework for debt recovery is in active flux as of November 2026 — the RBI has been moving from older, fragmented circulars toward a single consolidated "Responsible Business Conduct" (RBC) framework, with multiple draft and amendment versions issued through 2026. Dates, effective-from timelines, and even which rules are "final" vs "draft" are genuinely contested across sources below. **A qualified Indian financial-regulatory/fintech lawyer must verify current-in-force text before any product build**, especially given how recently (within the current year) major changes have been proposed/finalized.

---

## What does the RBI Fair Practices Code say about debt collection/recovery agent conduct (permitted calling hours, prohibition on harassment, communication frequency, disclosure requirements)?

### Takeaway
The RBI's long-standing Fair Practices Code (FPC) bars intimidation/harassment and sets an 8am–7pm calling window; this was reinforced and tightened by an August 2022 circular and is now being superseded/consolidated by a much more detailed 2026 "Responsible Business Conduct" (RBC) framework that adds explicit third-party-contact restrictions, call-recording/consent rules, and mandatory agent certification.

### Cited Findings
- RBI issued an August 12, 2022 circular (RBI/2022-23/108) on "Outsourcing of Financial Services – Responsibilities of Regulated Entities employing Recovery Agents," triggered by recovery agents deviating from existing outsourcing/conduct instructions — [RBI Press Release](https://website.rbi.org.in/web/rbi/-/press-releases/outsourcing-of-financial-services-responsibilities-of-regulated-entities-employing-recovery-agents-54205); [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/?p=831138)
- The 2022 instructions explicitly bar recovery agents from calling borrowers before 8am and after 7pm, and reiterate the no-intimidation rule: "their agents do not resort to intimidation or harassment of any kind, either verbal or physical, against any person in their debt collection efforts" — [Business Standard](https://business-standard.com/amp/article/finance/rbi-directs-loan-recovery-agents-not-to-intimidate-borrowers-no-calling-before-8am-after-7pm-122081201144_1.html); [DeccanHerald](https://www.deccanherald.com/business/rbi-directs-loan-recovery-agents-not-to-intimidate-borrowers-no-calling-before-8am-after-7pm-1135618.html)
- Before the 2022 circular, the 8am–7pm calling-hours restriction had only formally applied to Housing Finance Companies and microfinance lenders; the 2022 instructions extended equivalent treatment across commercial banks (incl. regional rural banks), cooperative banks, and non-banking lenders — [TheQuint/TheQuint-style summary via search synthesis, primary basis: RBI circular text referenced in secondary reporting] (treat as secondary characterization; primary circular not directly fetched)
- Older Fair Practices Code language (cited in a 2010 Parliament answer) prohibited "undue harassment like persistently bothering the borrowers at odd hours, use of muscle power for recovery of loans," along with threatening or anonymous calls and false representations — [eparlib.nic.in PDF](https://eparlib.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/591273/1/94901.pdf)
- Non-observance of RBI guidelines on engagement of recovery agents is a recognized ground of complaint under the Banking Ombudsman Scheme — [search synthesis citing RBI Banking Ombudsman Scheme provisions]
- RBI has since (2026) moved to consolidate/replace the older bank-only and HFC-only recovery-agent instructions into comprehensive directions covering **all** regulated entities (banks, NBFCs, HFCs, ARCs, AIFIs). A draft was issued, revised, and reportedly finalized in stages through 2026: a February 2026 draft, a May 2026 revised draft, and an August 6, 2026 set described by one source as final — [Vinod Kothari Consultants, Aug 2026](https://vinodkothari.com/2026/08/rbis-recovery-norms-allow-device-locking-by-lenders/); [Taxmann](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/rbi-proposes-stricter-norms-for-loan-recovery-and-recovery-agents/); [Vinod Kothari, Feb 2026](https://vinodkothari.com/2026/02/rbi-proposes-uniform-recovery-norms-across-all-lenders/)
- Per the Vinod Kothari August 2026 summary of the (apparently) finalized directions: recovery agents may contact/visit borrowers **only between 08:00 and 19:00 hours**; lenders must honor a borrower's specific request to avoid calls/visits at particular times; **"excessive or anonymous calls"** and intimidation/harassment are prohibited (no explicit numeric call-frequency cap found); field visits for microfinance loans are permitted only after repeated non-appearance by the borrower — [Vinod Kothari, Aug 2026](https://vinodkothari.com/2026/08/rbis-recovery-norms-allow-device-locking-by-lenders/)
- The same source states agents must deal **only with the borrower or guarantor**, not relatives or other third-party contacts (code-of-conduct restriction) — [Vinod Kothari, Aug 2026](https://vinodkothari.com/2026/08/rbis-recovery-norms-allow-device-locking-by-lenders/)
- Borrowers must be informed that calls are recorded; records of call timing, frequency, and recordings must be retained for **at least 6 months, or until any sub judice matter is disposed of** — [Vinod Kothari, Aug 2026](https://vinodkothari.com/2026/08/rbis-recovery-norms-allow-device-locking-by-lenders/)
- A Sansa Legal summary of a related draft ("Commercial Banks Responsible Business Conduct Second Amendment Directions 2026") states lenders must disclose a recovery agent's name, designation and contact details to the borrower on request, without delay, and must give prior written notice (email/SMS/registered post) before any in-person agent visit — [Sansa Legal](https://www.sansalegal.com/post/rbi-responsible-business-conduct-directions-2026-new-rules-on-debt-recovery-and-borrower-rights)
- NBFCs/REs must adopt a board-approved code of conduct for recovery agents and, where an external agency is used, obtain a written undertaking from that agency that its agents will comply with the code — [TaxGuru synthesis citing para 100J-style provisions]; older ARC-specific version at [RBI NBFC notification](https://rbi.org.in/SCRIPTs/BS_NBFCNotificationView.aspx?Id=11937)
- A separate, newer RBI provision (per Vinod Kothari, Aug 2026) permits lenders to **remotely restrict device features** (on loans that financed the device, with express contractual authorization) only gradually: partial restriction from 30 DPD, full restriction only at 60 DPD; outgoing calls cannot be restricted before 60 DPD; essential functions (internet, incoming calls, emergency SOS) can never be restricted; restrictions must be reversed within 1 hour of dues being cleared; NBFCs must pay ₹250/hour penalty (capped at loan amount) for wrongful/delayed restriction; lenders/vendors are barred from accessing or retaining borrower device data; the locking mechanism must be uninstalled after full repayment — [Vinod Kothari, Aug 2026](https://vinodkothari.com/2026/08/rbis-recovery-norms-allow-device-locking-by-lenders/)

### Inferences
- The regulatory trend is clearly toward *more* prescriptive, codified rules (specific hours, specific retention periods, specific device-locking mechanics) rather than the older, vaguer "no harassment" language — a compliance tooling vendor should design for granular, auditable rule enforcement (per-call timestamp logging, consent-recording, retention-period tagging) rather than just a basic time-window filter.
- The mandatory 6-month+ call-recording/metadata retention requirement (independent of DPDP retention rules) means any AI calling platform needs its own RBI-driven retention/discovery layer, separate from standard data-minimization practice.
- The "only borrower or guarantor, not third parties" rule is now being written explicitly into recovery-agent codes of conduct rather than left as informal practice — this is a hard technical requirement for any contact-list/dialer logic (see next section).

### Gaps
- I could not independently fetch or verify the **primary, final RBI circular/Master Direction text** (the RBI website's own press-release URL redirected to the RBI homepage rather than the specific document). All findings on the "finalized" August 2026 RBC directions come from secondary legal/compliance commentary (Vinod Kothari, Sansa Legal, Taxmann, TaxGuru), which themselves disagree on whether the August 2026 text is final or still draft, and on effective dates (some cite July 1, 2026 for banks, January 2027 for RRBs/AIFIs; one cites January 1, 2027 broadly). **This must be confirmed against rbi.org.in directly before product planning.**
- No source gave a precise numeric cap on calls-per-day/week (e.g., "max 3 calls/day") — only the qualitative "no excessive or anonymous calls" standard was found. If a specific numeric frequency cap exists in the final text, it was not located in this research pass.

## What do the RBI's Digital Lending Guidelines (2022 and updates) say about recovery agent engagement, outsourcing of collections, and borrower communication for digital/app-based loans specifically?

### Takeaway
The original September 2022 Digital Lending Guidelines required digital lenders to disclose recovery-agent identity to borrowers before any recovery contact and made clear that outsourcing collections to a Lending Service Provider (LSP) does not reduce the regulated entity's own liability; these guidelines have since been superseded by the RBI (Digital Lending) Directions, 2025, which reportedly address recovery more directly, but the primary text of the 2025 Directions was not retrieved in this research pass.

### Cited Findings
- RBI's original digital lending framework (Guidelines on Digital Lending, dated September 2, 2022) required that outsourcing arrangements between a Regulated Entity (RE) and a Lending Service Provider (LSP)/Digital Lending App (DLA) "does not diminish the REs' obligations," i.e., the RE remains fully responsible for recovery conduct even when it is outsourced/app-mediated — [secondary synthesis of RBI digital lending guidelines, via search]
- Digital lenders were required to communicate the particulars of any recovery agent to the borrower via email/SMS **before** that agent makes first contact for recovery, and the Key Fact Statement (KFS) for the loan must list details of the LSP authorized to approach the borrower for recovery — [search synthesis citing RBI digital lending FAQ/press coverage]
- Existing digital loans were required to be brought into compliance with the 2022 guidelines by November 30, 2022 — [search synthesis, secondary]
- The RBI (Digital Lending) Directions, 2025 **supersede and replace** the September 2, 2022 Guidelines on Digital Lending, and reportedly give more explicit treatment to recovery practices than the earlier version did — [JSA Prism FinTech, May 2025 note](https://www.jsalaw.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/JSA-Prism-FinTech-May-2025-Digital-Lending.Final.pdf) (referenced in search results; not independently fetched)

### Inferences
- For a digital-lending-focused AI collections vendor, the LSP/DLA disclosure requirement (recovery agent identity disclosed in advance via SMS/email, and listed in the KFS) implies that an AI voice-calling product serving digital lenders needs an upstream workflow step: generating and sending borrower-facing recovery-agent/channel disclosure *before* any automated call campaign starts, not just at loan origination.
- Because outsourcing doesn't reduce RE liability, any AI vendor's contract and system design should assume the lender (bank/NBFC) will demand full audit rights, logs, and the ability to demonstrate FPC/RBC compliance on the vendor's calling system — this is a sales/product requirement (compliance dashboards, exportable audit trails), not just a legal nicety.

### Gaps
- The actual text of the RBI (Digital Lending) Directions, 2025 was not retrieved — only a secondary reference to its existence and general effect. Specific recovery-related clauses in the 2025 Directions need direct verification from RBI's website.
- No source was found addressing whether digital lending's recovery-agent disclosure rule interacts differently with AI/automated calling versus human calling (e.g., does an AI agent count as a "recovery agent" for KFS-disclosure purposes?). This is an open compliance question with no clear regulatory answer found.

## Are there specific rules on recovery agent (DRA/BCSBI) certification, whether an AI voice agent can legally make collection calls, or whether a human must be in the loop at some point?

### Takeaway
RBI requires recovery agents to hold IIBF ("Indian Institute of Banking & Finance") Debt Recovery Agent (DRA) certification, with newer 2026 rules reportedly extending/tightening this and giving existing uncertified agents a one-year grace period; however, **no RBI rule was found that explicitly addresses AI voice agents** — the regulatory text is caller-conduct-based (what is said/done) rather than caller-identity-based (human vs. bot), meaning AI calling is not explicitly prohibited but also not explicitly blessed, and certification/human-in-loop requirements as currently written appear to assume a human agent.

### Cited Findings
- IIBF is described as currently the only body certifying Debt Recovery Agents (DRAs) in India, via a course of either 50 hours (graduates) or 100 hours (sub-graduate/10th-level candidates, per differing sources), delivered through accredited training institutes — [Careers360](https://www.careers360.com/courses-certifications/articles/iibf-dra-certificate); [IIBF DRA syllabus PDF](https://www.iibf.org.in/documents/RulesSyllabus/2024/DRA_Rev_030424.pdf); [IIBF FAQ for training institutes](https://beta.iibf.org.in/documents/FAQ_DRA_Training_Institutes.pdf)
- Banks/NBFCs are expected to engage only recovery personnel (including those sourced via DSAs/DMAs/DRAs) who have completed training and hold IIBF certification — [search synthesis]
- 2026 draft/final RBI provisions (per multiple secondary sources) make IIBF DRA certification a hard requirement for all recovery agents across all regulated entities, including coverage of SARFAESI and Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC) procedures and ethical-conduct standards; existing agents without certification get a one-year window from the directions' issuance to obtain it — [Vinod Kothari, Aug 2026](https://vinodkothari.com/2026/08/rbis-recovery-norms-allow-device-locking-by-lenders/); [Sansa Legal](https://www.sansalegal.com/post/rbi-responsible-business-conduct-directions-2026-new-rules-on-debt-recovery-and-borrower-rights); [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/?p=1066779)
- Lenders will reportedly be required to publish an up-to-date list of empanelled recovery agents/agencies, including individual agent names and engagement period — [search synthesis, Taxmann]
- No RBI circular, press release, or master direction explicitly naming "AI," "voice bot," "automated calling," or "chatbot" collections was found in this research. A vendor-marketing article (caller.digital) frames RBI's Fair Practices Code as applying "as much to automated calls as human ones" based on the conduct-based (not caller-type-based) drafting of the rules, but this is the vendor's own interpretation, not an RBI statement — [caller.digital](https://caller.digital/blog/rbi-questions-ai-voice-bot-collections-nbfc-india)
- The same vendor source claims RBI's broader "FREE-AI" framework (a generic RBI framework for responsible/ethical AI use by regulated entities, referenced but not independently verified here) includes expectations of "explainability, human-in-the-loop, and recourse" for AI deployed by regulated entities generally — [caller.digital](https://caller.digital/blog/rbi-questions-ai-voice-bot-collections-nbfc-india) — **unverified against primary RBI FREE-AI text in this pass**
- The same vendor source asserts that AI-bot disclosure to the borrower ("you are speaking with an automated system") "must be explicit, in the borrower's language, and at the start of every call" as a practical compliance reading of the Fair Practices Code — this is the vendor's interpretive recommendation, not a quoted RBI rule — [caller.digital](https://caller.digital/blog/rbi-questions-ai-voice-bot-collections-nbfc-india)
- Multiple AI-collections vendor marketing pages (Exotel, Oriserve, RevRag, Nugget, Mobicule) describe "best practice" human escalation design: bots should recognize disputes/hardship/grievance signals and hand off to a human agent with context, and several explicitly state later-stage delinquency (e.g., 91+ days past due) should stay with human specialists as a design choice, not a stated legal mandate — [Exotel](https://exotel.com/blog/outbound-voice-ai-compliance/); [Nugget](https://www.nugget.com/resources/ai-voicebots-debt-collection/) — these are vendor claims about their own product design, not confirmed regulatory mandates
- RBI inspection/examiner practice is characterized by one vendor source as not distinguishing "a human recovery agent using abusive language" from "a voice bot generating intimidatory [language]" — i.e., the behavioral standard (no intimidation, proper hours, proper disclosure) applies regardless of whether the caller is human or AI — [caller.digital](https://caller.digital/blog/rbi-questions-ai-voice-bot-collections-nbfc-india)

### Inferences
- Because RBI's rules are written in terms of *conduct outcomes* (no harassment, proper hours, proper disclosure, recording consent, agent identification) rather than *caller type*, there is a reasonable (but unconfirmed) basis to argue AI voice agents are not per se prohibited from making collection calls — but this is an interpretation, not a confirmed legal position, and should be tested with counsel, especially regarding whether an "agent" in RBI's text is implicitly assumed to be a natural person requiring IIBF certification (which is structured as a *personal* credential, hard to map onto a software agent).
- The IIBF certification regime, as currently designed, certifies *individual humans*. There is no evident mechanism for certifying an AI system equivalently. A practical/conservative compliance posture for a vendor would be: treat the AI as a *channel/tool used by* a certified human supervisor or escalation agent, with the certified human bearing ultimate accountability, rather than positioning the AI as an autonomous substitute for a certified DRA.
- The absence of any explicit RBI statement on AI voice bots (positive or negative) is itself a material finding: this is genuinely unsettled regulatory terrain in India as of late 2026, which is both a market opportunity (undefined, so early movers can shape "compliance by design" expectations) and a risk (no safe harbor; regulatory position could tighten post hoc, e.g., in a future FREE-AI-linked update).

### Gaps
- No primary RBI document addressing AI/automated voice calling for collections was found or fetched. The "RBI FREE-AI framework" reference is from a single vendor blog and was not independently verified — its existence, scope, and applicability to collections calling specifically should be confirmed directly (search for RBI's actual "Framework for Responsible and Ethical Enablement of AI" or similarly named initiative).
- No explicit human-in-the-loop *legal mandate* (as opposed to vendor best-practice/design choice) was confirmed for AI collections calling specifically.
- Whether a fully autonomous AI voice agent (no human escalation path at all) would be found non-compliant was not confirmed either way — this is an open question requiring direct legal opinion.

## What restrictions exist on contacting third parties (family, employer, references) during collections, and on calling frequency/time-of-day?

### Takeaway
Calling hours are restricted to roughly 8am–7pm (consistently cited across both older FPC-era rules and 2026 updates); third-party contact (family, friends, employer, neighbours, workplace colleagues) without the borrower's consent is restricted, with the narrow exception of a co-borrower/guarantor/co-applicant on the same loan, who may be contacted independently as a party to the loan.

### Cited Findings
- Recovery agents may contact/visit borrowers only between 08:00 and 19:00 hours (consistent across the 2022 circular reporting and 2026 draft/final summaries) — [Vinod Kothari, Aug 2026](https://vinodkothari.com/2026/08/rbis-recovery-norms-allow-device-locking-by-lenders/); [Sansa Legal](https://www.sansalegal.com/post/rbi-responsible-business-conduct-directions-2026-new-rules-on-debt-recovery-and-borrower-rights); [Business Standard, 2022](https://business-standard.com/amp/article/finance/rbi-directs-loan-recovery-agents-not-to-intimidate-borrowers-no-calling-before-8am-after-7pm-122081201144_1.html)
- Recovery agents must deal only with the borrower or the guarantor; they are not permitted to approach relatives, friends, neighbours, or workplace colleagues about the debt without the borrower's consent — [Vinod Kothari, Aug 2026](https://vinodkothari.com/2026/08/rbis-recovery-norms-allow-device-locking-by-lenders/); secondary consumer-rights summaries (e.g., [GoCredit blog](https://gocredit.money/loan-kavach/recovery-harassment/rbi-guidelines-for-loan-recovery-agents)) echo the same point but as consumer-facing commentary, not primary text
- A co-guarantor or co-applicant of the *same loan* is treated as a party to the loan and may be contacted independently of the primary borrower — [search synthesis, secondary consumer-rights sources]
- Agents are not permitted to use a borrower's phone contact list/phonebook to reach third parties without explicit, purpose-specific consent — [search synthesis, secondary]
- Prior written notice (via email, SMS, or registered post) is required before any in-person recovery-agent visit, which is framed by one legal commentary as specifically preventing unannounced workplace visits/ambushes — [Sansa Legal](https://www.sansalegal.com/post/rbi-responsible-business-conduct-directions-2026-new-rules-on-debt-recovery-and-borrower-rights)
- No numeric cap on calls-per-day was found in any source; the standard found is qualitative ("no excessive or anonymous calls") — [Vinod Kothari, Aug 2026](https://vinodkothari.com/2026/08/rbis-recovery-norms-allow-device-locking-by-lenders/)
- One news source noted a possible contact-at-workplace allowance if the borrower is otherwise unreachable — this appears to refer to contacting the *borrower themselves* at their workplace, not contacting the employer about the debt, but the distinction was not confirmed cleanly across sources and should be checked against primary text — [search synthesis, Upstox article referenced in search results]

### Inferences
- For AI calling-system design, this translates into concrete, enforceable technical controls: (1) a hard time-window gate (localize to borrower's time zone/region, 8am–7pm), (2) a contact-role tag on every phone number in the system (borrower / guarantor-coborrower / other-do-not-call), with automated blocking of any outbound call to a number tagged as a third party not party to the loan, (3) no auto-dialing based on phone/contact-list scraping without separate, purpose-specific consent capture, and (4) a frequency/anti-"excessive calling" throttle, even absent a specific numeric regulatory cap, since "excessive" is an undefined but enforceable standard that regulators/ombudsman complaints could invoke.

### Gaps
- No clear, confirmed primary-source statement on whether "references" supplied at loan application time may be contacted during collections (as distinct from guarantors) — this was flagged as a plausible-but-unconfirmed reading in secondary sources (e.g., lender blog templates) and needs direct legal confirmation.
- No confirmed rule on whether/how an employer may ever legitimately be contacted (e.g., for salary-attachment/wage-garnishment legal processes under separate law) as opposed to informal "calling the employer to pressure the borrower," which is prohibited. This distinction matters for product design and was not resolved by sources found.

## What data privacy/consent requirements apply (e.g. DPDP Act 2023) to storing and processing borrower contact data and call recordings for collections purposes?

### Takeaway
The DPDP Act 2023 treats collections call recordings and contact data as personal data requiring purpose-specific consent (separate from RBI/TRAI call-consent concepts), with the lender as the primary liable "Data Fiduciary" even when calling is outsourced to a vendor processor; DPDP's substantive penalty regime is reported (with conflicting dates across sources) to come into force sometime in 2026–2027, while RBI separately mandates its own minimum 6-month retention period for recordings, meaning a compliant system must satisfy both frameworks concurrently, not either one in isolation.

### Cited Findings
- Collections call logs/recordings are characterized as "personal data" under the DPDP Act's scope, and most recovery operations fall within the Act's definition of "processing" — [King Stubb & Kasiva, referenced via search synthesis, specific article not independently fetched]; [Mondaq overview](https://www.mondaq.com/india/privacy-protection/1740018/data-privacy-compliance-in-digital-lending-financial-services)
- A LiveLaw legal-analysis piece argues call-centre/collections consent needs to be mapped to purpose: if the call's purpose is narrowly the stated enquiry, the Section 7(a) "legitimate use" pathway may apply rather than full consent-based processing, but data collected must be limited to what's necessary for that stated purpose (data minimization) — [LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/articles/dpdpa-says-about-call-centre-data-548760)
- Under the DPDP framework, outsourcing collections/call-centre work to a third-party agency does not shift primary legal liability away from the lender (data fiduciary); the processor relationship does not absolve the fiduciary — [search synthesis, multiple vendor/law-firm sources converge on this point, consistent with general DPDP structure]
- One AI-calling vendor (caller.digital) argues that TRAI consent (right to call a number, under telecom/DND regulation) and DPDP consent (right to record/store/process what happens on the call) are **legally distinct** and both must be independently obtained — recording should not be bundled into general loan-paperwork consent but captured as its own purpose-specific consent record — [caller.digital, vendor blog](https://caller.digital/blog/dpdp-act-compliance-checklist-voice-ai-india) — vendor's own interpretive framing, not a quoted regulator position
- RBI call-recording retention requirements (separate from DPDP) are reported by the same vendor source as a 6-month floor for recordings and a 5-year retention period for KYC-related data — this echoes the 6-month figure independently found in the Vinod Kothari recovery-directions summary above, lending it some cross-source credibility, though the KYC 5-year figure wasn't independently cross-checked — [caller.digital](https://caller.digital/blog/dpdp-act-compliance-checklist-voice-ai-india); corroborating 6-month figure also at [Vinod Kothari, Aug 2026](https://vinodkothari.com/2026/08/rbis-recovery-norms-allow-device-locking-by-lenders/)
- A Khaitan & Co. legal analysis states the two regimes serve different purposes and must both be satisfied: DPDP governs how personal data is collected/used/shared/retained/erased, while RBI's (now-RBC) directions govern fair offering/sale of financial products and require their own disclosure formats (e.g., Key Facts Statements) — satisfying DPDP consent does not automatically satisfy RBI disclosure/suitability standards, and vice versa — [Khaitan & Co. Compass](https://compass.khaitanco.com/consent-architecture-in-financial-services-distribution-comparing-rbis-responsible-business-conduct-directions-and-the-dpdp-framework)
- Per the same Khaitan & Co. piece: RBI's Responsible Business Conduct (Second Amendment) Directions, 2026 were issued June 15, 2026 and take effect January 1, 2027; the DPDP framework is reported to become fully operational May 13, 2027 — [Khaitan & Co. Compass](https://compass.khaitanco.com/consent-architecture-in-financial-services-distribution-comparing-rbis-responsible-business-conduct-directions-and-the-dpdp-framework)
- A conflicting source (ICSI publication, via search synthesis) separately cites the DPDP "penalty-based liability regime" coming into force on May 13, 2027, broadly consistent with Khaitan's date, while a Lawxy article instead cites enforcement of specific DPDP sections (6(9) and 27(1)(d)) from November 13, 2026 — these dates were not reconciled and should be verified directly against the official DPDP Rules 2025 notification — [search synthesis]
- Under the RBI directions (per Khaitan & Co.), consent interfaces must default to "No"/non-agreement, dark patterns (basket-sneaking, forced action, subscription traps) are prohibited, unsubscribe from promotional messaging must be simple, and product-related consent records must be retained for one year after the contractual relationship ends — [Khaitan & Co. Compass](https://compass.khaitanco.com/consent-architecture-in-financial-services-distribution-comparing-rbis-responsible-business-conduct-directions-and-the-dpdp-framework)

### Inferences
- A compliant AI collections platform needs a dual-track consent/retention architecture: (1) a DPDP-compliant consent record (purpose-limited, specific to call recording/processing, separately revocable) layered on top of (2) RBI-mandated minimum retention (≥6 months for recordings, apparently longer for KYC-linked data) — these are not substitutable; satisfying one does not satisfy the other, and a vendor must build logging/retention logic that tracks both simultaneously, including handling the tension between DPDP's "erase when purpose is fulfilled / on consent withdrawal" principle and RBI's mandatory minimum retention floor (regulatory retention mandates typically override consumer erasure requests in Indian data law practice, but this specific interaction for collections recordings was not confirmed in sources found).
- Given the real-world effective-date uncertainty for both frameworks (various 2026/2027 dates cited), a vendor building "for the future" should assume both frameworks will be in force well within a typical 12–24 month product build/sales cycle, so compliance-by-design should not be deferred as a "phase 2" feature.

### Gaps
- The precise statutory DPDP Act section numbers governing consent notices, the "significant data fiduciary" threshold (which may apply to a large-scale AI collections vendor processing data for many lenders), and cross-border data processing/storage rules relevant to a SaaS vendor's cloud infrastructure were not retrieved in this pass — this needs direct review of the DPDP Act 2023 text and DPDP Rules 2025.
- No source definitively reconciled the conflicting DPDP enforcement dates (May 13, 2027 vs. November 13, 2026 for specific sections) — flagged explicitly as unresolved.

## What do existing Indian collections-tech vendors say publicly about building "compliance-by-design" into their calling systems?

### Takeaway
Named incumbents (Credgenics, DPDzero) and several AI-voice vendors market "compliance by design" features — DND/calling-window enforcement, consent logging, data encryption/anonymization, ISO 27001 certification, audit trails, and human-escalation workflows — but nearly all of this evidence is vendor self-reporting/marketing with no independent verification or confirmed regulatory audit found; no claims from Gnani.ai, Spocto X, Rezolv, SquadStack, or Trustt were surfaced in this research pass.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- The competitive/market framing suggests that "compliance by design" is already a marketed differentiator in this space, not a novel pitch — a new entrant would need genuinely verifiable (ideally third-party-audited) compliance claims to differentiate, since every incumbent found makes similar self-reported claims (DND/time-window enforcement, consent logging, encryption) without visible independent certification specific to collections-calling compliance (ISO 27001 is a general infosec certification, not an RBI-collections-specific one).
- The "material outsourcing" framing is a meaningful sales consideration: if true, lenders will demand board-reportable audit rights and formal outsourcing-agreement terms from any AI vendor, which has implications for contract structure, not just product features.
